How it works

The car runs on one physics simulation, written in Rust, that is shared by the game and by training. It has ten nozzles on a compressed-air tank: four lift the corners, four push the sides and two fire backwards. In Realistic mode a full second of lift empties a third of the tank.

A classical PD controller levels the car, brakes its fall and rights it on the ground. The AI is a small neural network trained with PPO to save upsets: flips, rolls and tumbles that PD cannot catch.

"PD + AI" gives the AI the nozzles only when the car tips past 60°, and hands them back to PD once it is settled on its wheels. The AI was trained under that same rule, so it practised exactly the flights it gets here.

Inspired by reporting on the October 2026 thruster-car demo: TechCrunch, Glitchwire.
